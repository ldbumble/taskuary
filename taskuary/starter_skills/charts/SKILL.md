# Turn data into charts

A good chart makes one comparison obvious in a few seconds, and its title says what to see.

## Before charting

Look at the data first: what each column is, its units, the period covered, gaps and odd values. Decide what question the chart answers. If the owner did not say and the data could answer several, ask which matters - once.

## Choosing and drawing

- Change over time: a line. Sizes compared: bars, sorted, starting at zero. Parts of a whole: stacked bars, or a pie only for a few slices. Two measures against each other: a scatter.
- One message a chart. Several messages are several small charts, not one crowded one.
- The title states the finding ("Refunds doubled after the price change"); the axes are labelled with units; the source and the dates are under it.
- Label lines and bars directly where there is room, rather than sending the eye to a legend. Colour marks what matters; everything else is quiet.
- No 3D, no decoration, no second axis unless nothing else works - and then say so.
- Never tidy the data silently. Rows left out, values filled in or outliers removed are stated beside the chart.

## Finishing

Check each chart against the numbers it came from: pick two points and confirm them by hand. Show the charts to the owner, with a sentence each on what they show and anything in the data they should know about, and hand over the picture files and the figures behind them.
