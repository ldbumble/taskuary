# Build a small website

A good small site says who it is for, what is offered and what to do next on its first screen, and reads well on a phone.

## Ask first

Before building anything, interview the owner: ask what only they know, all of it at once as one set of questions, never one at a time and never as a paragraph of prose.

1. Who is the site for, and what should a visitor do there (call, visit, order, book, sign up)?
2. Which pages does it need - one page, or home, about, menu or services, contact?
3. What tone - warm, plain, playful, formal?
4. Brand - colours, a logo, a typeface, or a site whose look they like?
5. What content do they already have - text, photos, prices, opening hours, an address?
6. Is there a domain, and where will it be hosted - or are files enough for now?

Offer a few likely answers where a pick is quicker than typing. Where they leave one empty, choose something sensible and say what you chose.

## Build

- Plain HTML and CSS in one folder that opens by double-clicking `index.html`. No framework and no build step unless they asked for one.
- Their own words and facts. Where content is missing, a clearly marked placeholder - never an invented phone number, price, review or opening hour.
- Phone first: one column, large type, buttons a thumb can hit; then wider screens.
- A few colours and one or two typefaces, used the same way on every page. Every picture has alt text, and text has enough contrast to read.
- The contact details and the one thing a visitor should do are on every page.

## Check and hand over

Open it in the browser and look, at phone width and at desktop width: every page, every link, nothing cut off or overflowing. Show the owner the home page. Then hand over the folder, saying in a few lines what is in it, which placeholders wait for their content, and how it goes online with the hosting they named.
