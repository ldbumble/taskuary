# Make a short deck

A good deck makes one argument. Read only its slide titles, top to bottom, and you should have the whole story.

## Before building

Know the audience, what they should think or decide at the end, and how long the owner has to speak. Ask if the task leaves these open - and whether there is a template or brand to follow.

## Building it

- Write the titles first, as full sentences that each make a point. If the titles do not tell the story, fix the story before making slides.
- One point a slide. A few words, or one chart, or one picture - not all three. What the speaker will say goes in the notes, not on the slide.
- Fewer slides than seems natural: about one for every two minutes of talking. The first says what this is about and what is being asked; the last says what happens next.
- Numbers are the owner's own, with their source and date in small type. One number a slide is remembered; ten are not.
- One layout, one typeface, a few colours, kept the same throughout. Text large enough to read from the back of a room.

## Finishing

Look at every slide as a picture: nothing cut off, nothing overlapping, nothing too small. Hand over a file they can open and edit, and say what you assumed and where a figure or a name still needs their check.
