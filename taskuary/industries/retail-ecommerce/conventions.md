# How the work runs - retail & e-commerce

Retail calendars and routines differ by company, product category, channel and country. These are common patterns, not universal deadlines.

## Every day
- **Sales and traffic** - teams check sales, order volume, visits, conversion and exceptions against a target or recent baseline.
- **Inventory** - low-stock items, stockouts, order queues and fulfilment exceptions are reviewed.
- **Customer service** - enquiries, cancellations, refunds, returns and payment disputes are handled.
- **Store operations** - physical stores manage opening and closing routines, staffing and point-of-sale issues.

## Every month
- **Month-end close** - sales, refunds, discounts, payment fees and inventory movements are reconciled under company procedures.
- **Performance review** - teams review product and channel performance, margin, markdowns, returns and stock ageing.
- **Buying and replenishment** - forecasts, purchase orders, supplier follow-ups and stock plans are updated.
- **Campaign reporting** - marketing and e-commerce teams review campaign results and customer-service trends.

## Every quarter
- **Business review** - performance is compared with budget and forecast.
- **Assortment and supplier review** - teams reassess products, pricing, promotions, supplier performance and inventory exposure.
- **Public-company reporting** - listed companies may publish quarterly results on their own investor-relations schedules.
- **Tax and compliance checks** - obligations depend on the business and jurisdiction; use the relevant official schedule.

## Every year
- **Holiday season** - many retailers plan promotions, staffing, inventory, shipping capacity and returns around major shopping periods.
- **Back-to-school** - important for some categories and regions; dates depend on local school calendars.
- **Black Friday / Cyber Monday** - major shopping events in the U.S. and some other markets. Dates change each year because they are tied to Thanksgiving.
- **Regional and cultural events** - Lunar New Year, Ramadan/Eid, Diwali and other events may affect demand, hours, staffing and shipping in relevant markets.
- **Seasonal assortment changes** - apparel and other categories may plan ranges and markdowns around spring/summer or autumn/winter, depending on the market.
- **Fiscal year-end** - may trigger inventory counts, financial close, budget planning and annual reporting. A fiscal year does not always match the calendar year.
- **4-5-4 retail calendar** - some retailers use a 4-5-4 week pattern to compare similar retail periods. NRF publishes a voluntary guide; check the company's own calendar and any 53-week-year adjustment.

## Events that drive a lot of mail
- A promotion, campaign, product launch or price change is about to start or end.
- A supplier delay, purchase-order issue, stockout or inventory discrepancy needs attention.
- A marketplace changes a listing, seller policy or fulfilment requirement.
- A carrier delay, fulfilment disruption or returns spike affects customer orders.
- A store opens, closes, remodels or changes operating hours.
- A financial close, budget cycle, audit request or earnings announcement creates a deadline.
- A product recall, safety issue or regulatory notice affects a product or sales channel.

## How to read the mail
- A subject with a SKU, purchase order or product name often concerns inventory, buying, listing or fulfilment.
- A message about conversion, AOV, GMV or traffic is usually a sales or digital performance report; check the metric definition and period.
- A carrier exception or order-management alert may require operational follow-up.
- A regulator or tax authority message with a deadline is a task, not an FYI; verify it through the official channel.

For a date, legal requirement, tax question or accounting treatment, check the current official source and the organisation's own policy before acting.
