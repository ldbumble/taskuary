# Glossary - retail & e-commerce

## Sales, volume and money
- **GMV** - gross merchandise value: the value of merchandise sold or transacted through a platform over a stated period. Platforms may differ in how they treat cancellations, returns, tax, shipping and fees.
- **Net sales** - sales after deductions specified by the company's reporting policy, commonly including returns, allowances and discounts.
- **AOV** - average order value: order revenue divided by the number of orders over a stated period. Check what the report includes in revenue.
- **ASP** - average selling price: sales value divided by units sold over a stated period.
- **Comparable sales / comps** - sales change for a defined group of stores or locations included in both comparison periods. Eligibility rules and adjustments vary by company.
- **Gross margin** - sales less cost of goods sold, often expressed as a percentage of sales. Check the accounting definition and included costs.
- **Markup** - the amount added to a product's cost to set its selling price. Markup percentage and gross-margin percentage are different measures.
- **Markdown** - a reduction from a previous or regular selling price.
- **Sell-through** - the share of a defined inventory quantity sold during a stated period. Check the denominator and time window.
- **Inventory turnover** - a measure of how often inventory is sold or used during a period. One common calculation uses cost of goods sold divided by average inventory.

## Customers and digital
- **Conversion rate** - the share of defined visits, sessions, leads or other opportunities that result in a specified action. Check the denominator and conversion event.
- **Traffic / sessions** - visits or browsing sessions recorded by a store or digital analytics system. Measurement rules differ by platform.
- **CTR** - click-through rate: clicks divided by a defined number of impressions, usually expressed as a percentage.
- **CAC** - customer acquisition cost: a measure of the cost of acquiring customers over a stated period. The included costs and attribution method vary.
- **CLV / LTV** - customer lifetime value: an estimate of the value associated with a customer relationship over time. Methods and assumptions vary.
- **Repeat purchase rate** - the share of a defined customer group that makes more than one purchase during the stated period.
- **Abandoned cart** - a cart containing items that the customer did not complete into an order, according to the platform's tracking rules.
- **DTC** - direct-to-consumer: a brand or business sells directly to customers rather than only through other retailers.
- **Marketplace** - a platform where multiple sellers offer products to customers. The platform may also handle payments, fulfilment or customer service.
- **Omnichannel** - coordinated operations and customer experience across channels such as stores, websites, apps and marketplaces.

## Products, inventory and fulfilment
- **SKU** - stock keeping unit: an identifier a business uses to distinguish an inventory item or variant in its own system.
- **UPC / GTIN** - product identification standards used in commerce. An internal SKU is not interchangeable with a GTIN.
- **Assortment** - the set of products and variants a retailer offers in a store, channel or category.
- **On hand** - inventory recorded as physically available at a location; system quantities may need reconciliation.
- **ATP** - available to promise: the quantity a system indicates can be promised to customers under the organisation's inventory and allocation rules.
- **Stockout** - a situation where an item is unavailable when demand occurs.
- **Replenishment** - restocking products to maintain or restore availability.
- **Lead time** - time between defined points, such as placing a purchase order and receiving goods.
- **PO** - purchase order: a buyer's formal request for specified goods or services under stated terms.
- **3PL** - third-party logistics provider: an outside company that performs services such as warehousing, fulfilment or transportation.
- **Fulfilment** - preparing and delivering an order, often including picking, packing and dispatch.
- **BOPIS / click and collect** - buy online, pick up in store; customers place an online order and collect it at a store or designated location.
- **Reverse logistics** - the movement and handling of goods coming back through returns, repairs, recycling or disposal.
- **Shrink** - inventory loss or discrepancy, which may result from theft, damage, administrative errors or other causes.

## Orders, payments and systems
- **POS** - point of sale: checkout and the associated hardware or software used to record a sale.
- **OMS** - order management system: software that tracks and coordinates orders across channels and fulfilment locations.
- **ERP** - enterprise resource planning: software connecting business processes such as finance, purchasing, inventory and operations.
- **PIM** - product information management: software used to maintain product data for use across sales channels.
- **CRM** - customer relationship management: software for recording and managing customer interactions and related sales or service activity.
- **Payment gateway** - a service that securely passes payment information between checkout and payment-processing systems.
- **Chargeback** - a payment dispute process in which a cardholder challenges a transaction through their payment provider.
- **Return rate** - the share of orders or items returned under a defined calculation and time window.
- **Refund** - money returned to a customer after a purchase, according to the seller's policy and applicable rules.
- **RMA** - return merchandise authorisation: a reference or approval used by some businesses to identify and manage a return.
- **SLA** - service-level agreement: an agreement defining expected service levels, such as response time or availability.
- **EDI** - electronic data interchange: the exchange of structured business documents between organisations, such as purchase orders and invoices.

## Planning and reporting
- **COGS** - cost of goods sold: the cost assigned to goods sold during a reporting period under the organisation's accounting policy.
- **DIO** - days inventory outstanding: a measure estimating how long inventory is held before sale, based on a stated calculation and period.
- **OTB** - open to buy: a merchandising budget or buying allowance used to plan further inventory purchases against planned sales, inventory and commitments.
- **Sell-in / sell-out** - sell-in usually means a supplier's sales to a retailer; sell-out usually means the retailer's sales to end customers.
- **Seasonality** - recurring changes in demand or operations associated with seasons, holidays, weather or other calendar patterns.
- **4-5-4 calendar** - a retail calendar that groups weeks into periods of four, five and four weeks to help compare similar retail periods. Not all retailers use it.
- **Fiscal year** - the 12-month period an organisation uses for financial reporting; it may not match the calendar year.

Metric formulas and terms vary by company, channel and market. When reading a report, check its definition, period, units and denominator.
