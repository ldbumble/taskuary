The Advisor

The Advisor is the one of Taskuary's three models that thinks about your work as a whole. Triage judges each arrival and the assistant moves things on a turn; the Advisor reads everything, on a clock, and says what should happen next: what is late, what nobody followed up, which step a mail served, what worked, which system is worth connecting. It never acts. It leaves a line, an idea row or a proposal, and you or the assistant do the rest. COUNSEL.md is its voice and the assistant's both.

The operational side - the report that runs it, its schedule, the five words under an idea - is on Reports and the Advisor. This page is what goes into it, and what it learns from.

What it reads#

What one run of the Advisor report hands the model, today:

Input What it tells the Advisor
SOUL.md and JOB.md, the head of each Who you are and what matters: it weighs a slipped thread by whom it serves, and never contradicts the constitution. The chat reads both whole; the report takes the opening of each
COUNSEL.md How it speaks and how readily it takes a position
Your work as the hub sees it The threads of the last two days with your own lines marked, what arrived, the open tasks and what closed this week, who is out of office from their auto-replies
The calendar Who you meet and when: context for a meeting, and a chase that should wait for the one on Thursday
The knowledge base What agents have learned, by name, so it does not raise what is already known
The sources it watches The systems and saved data views on its report card, pulled silently each run
Its own notes What it reviewed last time and what you put down, so it never raises the same silence twice
LEARNED.md's promoted lines What your own verdicts taught, with their counts - the habits it weighs, never restates as news
The outcome ledger The last month of what came of actions, each with an id to cite, and the ledger's page on each person the run names

Two more inputs reach the Advisor at reflection, the pass that rewrites LEARNED.md, rather than on a report run: LEARNED.md itself, whole, hypotheses included, the outcome ledger below, and what you pressed while its line was shown. A matching playbook reaches its line on an item (below); your mutes are designed as an input and not yet read.

None of this is copied into a prompt you cannot see. The report's Preview shows exactly what one run hands the model.

Two clocks#

On the clock, today. The Advisor report runs on its schedule and when the app opens; the morning brief runs daily; reflection runs at launch and after three corrections, and reads the ledger whenever it runs. These find what only a window shows: a promise gone quiet, a task nobody touched, a goal with no movement, a sender you keep hearing from through a system you have not connected. Their findings are rows, judged like any arrival, because a forgotten task is work waiting on you and belongs on the rail where Next reads it in turn. Nothing fires for absence; something has to look.

A rule worth making. When your own presses already draw a hard edge, the clock asks once whether to make it a rule, phrased as a question on its own idea row: "You've put down all 12 mails from newsletter@vendor.example and acted on none. Make it a rule so they are filed without reaching you? (Rules run before the AI.)" Make it a rule writes the rule under Settings → Rules, named "(learned)" and saying it was promoted from learning; Not ours is the no, and the same question comes back only once the evidence has tripled. The bars are counts, never a reading: eight threads from one sender put down (Not ours, Not a task, Ignore this sender) and none taken up is an ignore rule; twenty from an automated sender is a skip; three automated senders on one domain with twelve between them is a domain rule; five of one sender's tasks marked urgent by hand, none ever put down, is an escalate rule. Never an ignore or a skip for a colleague or for anyone you have written to (an escalate rule may be - it is for exactly those people, the boss above all), never when a rule already covers them, and one such question at a time.

On the event. The same brain also runs when one item changes: an agent stops or asks (or was cut off by Taskuary closing, which the next launch reports), a reply arrives on a watched thread, a mail is attached to a task, a pull request opens, a task closes, a due day is missed, a report lands - and every item the walk or the chat puts on the table, an email, a chat line from WhatsApp or Teams, a task, a goal or one of the assistant's own ideas, when no event has judged it yet. One small prompt on the light model answers with one verb from the actions the item's row already draws and one line, or nothing. Code checks the verb is one the row offers; a verb it does not is dropped and noted. What the prompt carries, each part bounded and the whole capped at 9,000 characters (whole sections drop from the end, the item and its verbs never):

Part What it is
The item Its title, who, when, why, channel and where it stands - and the task's due date, priority, remind date and done-when
The verbs offered The row's own buttons, the fix for a failed send under the name of the place it opens, and New task to fix this when nothing here fixes a failure
What failed A send refused or unconfirmed, a draft that could not be written, an act that errored, an agent run that failed - in plain words, with its reason
The draft The draft waiting for your yes, word for word, and what is attached to it
The thread The last three messages on it, theirs and yours, any channel
Possible duplicates Other open tasks with the same sender, thread or subject - and whether an agent is already on one
Your notes Standing notes and past verdicts about the sender or topic, and your corrections of triage there - the same evidence triage is handed
The agent What it last said, what it is asking, how its run ended, and mail that came in since its last turn
The goal or the idea A goal's open steps, who holds each, its day and how late, and when anything last moved. An idea's words, and for a follow-up how long the person has been quiet against their own day
LEARNED.md ## Your people, ## What you care about, ## How you work and ## Promises - the lines in use, closest to this sender first, within its budget
The ledger Outcome rows on the item, and what came of each verb offered with this sender before - evidence it weighs, never a line it says
The playbook, SOUL.md, JOB.md The matching playbook, and the opening of each document
The bigger read For the item on the table: what the hub knows about the people on it, and the month's outcomes

Its rules name the moments it used to get wrong: an agent run that failed is a failure, never a Continue into the same wall; a send refused for its address is fixed at the address, never redrafted; a draft that promises what nothing shows (a payment date, a file that is not attached) is not sent; work an agent can do, like an issue with a traceback, goes to an agent; someone quiet past their day is chased, where the row draws Chase.

What it knows about how you work is a reason too, said in the same one line beside the lit button, and only when it is a fact it can show. Each is counted from what you did, and Why? lists the acts it rests on:

  • How soon you answer someone. Once you have answered a person three times, an ask of theirs that has waited well past your usual says so: You usually answer Erin within the hour; this is 3h. Inside your usual, or for someone you have not answered yet, there is no line.
  • Whom you hand that kind of ask to. The Advisor reads whom you handed a sender's asks to before; when this ask is the same kind of work, it lights Hand it to a person with Ray handles payroll for you. What the ask is about is the model's to read, never a count's. Once you have handed a sender's asks to the same person twice, Hand it to a person opens with that person already in To, saying whose usual they are. They are chosen, not sent: nothing goes until you press Send it.
  • The day you put it off to. When you keep putting a sender's mail off to the same weekday, the memory writes it as a line under How you work in LEARNED.md, such as You put mail from renewals@payworth.example off to Friday. It climbs the same ladder as any other line: it waits in Watching, and is in use after three put-offs over two weeks. Remind me reads its day from that line. It opens with that day first, filled, saying whose habit it is, and the line shows on hover. Reword the day in the line and Remind me follows. Delete the line, or mark it Wrong, and there is no learned day. Nothing is set until you press it.
  • A person's own day. Chase lights after three working days; once three of someone's answers say how soon they answer, it lights on their day instead: Gail usually answers in a day; this is 3 days.

Those findings are never rows. They attach to the item at its revision and show when the walk reaches it, as one line in the strip at the foot of the canvas, above the action row, and the advised button on the row is the one dark button - the row's own, pressed through its own handler. Why? beside the line says what it rests on. A changed item drops its old line by one comparison. The rail's order does not read advice, by construction: the test that pins it builds the rail with and without advice and asks for the same order. Two settings govern it, in Settings → Advisor: the switch, and a cap on judgements per hour.

What you pressed. Every press on the row, and Next, is kept: the move it lit, the one you pressed, and what kind of item it was from whom. It is a record, and nothing is learned from it: a button press is not a fact about how you work, and a count of them once lit the same button on two asks from one domain that had nothing else in common. What LEARNED.md knows comes from what you did, in context - who wrote, what they are to you, how soon you answered, what you put off and until when, whom you handed it to.

What it brings up is yours to teach. The clock reads what you took up and what you said no to: the ideas you dismissed, and What to bring up in LEARNED.md. An idea of a kind you said no to again and again stays unsaid, however it is worded - three "unsubscribe from this newsletter" ideas put down, and the fourth newsletter gets none. A promise you keep on a schedule - the same person, the same weekday, three weeks or more - is raised when its day has passed with nothing sent to them: The headcount sheet you send Gail on Mondays hasn't gone yet. Anything you send them puts it down, and so does No, for that week.

How it is tested. tests/advisor_cases.py is the eval: every state the demo and the advisor matrix hold, plus a few they lack, each with the moves that are right there, the ones that never are, and what its prompt must carry. It runs in the suite against the demo's scripted Advisor. tests/rail_advisor_cases.py is the clock's: every kind of idea it puts on the rail (a follow-up, a promise, work gone quiet, the app's health, a system worth connecting, its own idea, a rule worth making), each in a world where it should fire and in worlds where it should not, plus the demo world five days on, checked twice with every line put down between. A line that fires where it should not, or fires and is not useful - about automated mail, back after a No, too early, the wrong person, stale, over an agent at work - fails the suite with its reason.

Seeing what it did. The clock menu on the stage opens the Advisor's notes for the item on the table: a receipt, not a transcript. Each entry says the event, what it decided (the pill and the line) or what it dropped and why (nothing to say, a verb the item did not carry, the hour's cap, no brain), and the evidence it cited by id - what it cited, not everything it read. "Why?" in the chat is answered from the same line.

What was not followed up#

A thread you wrote on that nobody answered, or a step somebody owes past its day, is slipped once it has been quiet past that person's usual day: half again their median answer, once the ledger has seen them answer three times, and the default from Settings → Advisor until then. Someone whose auto-reply says they are away never slips. A slipped row sits inside Tasks, the pipe count says "3 slipped", and when Next brings it up the card offers four answers, each an outcome the ledger keeps:

  • Chase them drafts a short nudge on the thread, in your voice, for your yes - copying whoever the ledger has seen get them to answer, when it has. When it goes out, the reply is marked a chase, and how long the answer took is counted against them.
  • Let it go puts it down: the step is dropped, or the follow-up closed. Three Let-it-gos on one person and the Advisor stops raising that person; nothing else changes.
  • They answered elsewhere closes the step as done (its box ticks) and keeps where, in your words.
  • Remind me is the same Remind me as everywhere else.

A reply arriving ends it on its own: the follow-up closes and the step leaves the lane. The morning brief carries the same facts, one line each, with the person's day named.

A page per person#

Facts about other people go to a page per person, derived from the ledger at reflection and kept under Settings → Docs → People by address: how fast they answer, whether a chase is needed, who on copy moves them, what they deliver late - each line with the evidence ids it rests on, and the counted numbers underneath. The lines are the model's reading; the numbers are the ledger's. Your own lines above the block are yours and survive every recompile. The page is read wherever the hub's dossier is read: the reply drafter, triage's context, meeting prep, and the Advisor's bigger read.

Facts about kinds of work go to the playbook they belong to, as a proposal: once a playbook has five closes on the ledger, reflection writes a bullet block - how often a close came open again, and how often the ones closed with a question still open did - and it waits for your yes on the Review queue. Nothing edits a playbook on its own; your own lines on it are never touched.

The outcome ledger#

Every store Taskuary keeps is written from one signal: your click or your sentence, at the moment you give it. LEARNED.md is what you do when you correct; the verdict and routing memories stop at the verdict. Nothing recorded what you did next, or what the world did after Taskuary acted. That is what the ledger records: one row per action the app took or proposed, filled in two halves.

The action, recorded when it happens What the sweep looks for Verdict
A reply sent (approved as drafted, or edited first) More material mail from them on the thread in a week. An auto-reply and a calendar response are not rounds; your own later mail is not theirs settled · unsettled: N more
A task closed, by any road Is it still closed a week on held · reopened
An agent stopped (with or without a pull request open) Your next move: a continue, with the words you typed into the pane or the session, or a close; or nothing in two days continued · closed · left
A pull request merged Two weeks on, nothing says otherwise held
A promise you made in a sent mail, as a task on you with its day Delivered when, against the day you named; or a week past it with nothing kept · kept late · late
A sender ignored by a rule Whether their mail became a task anyway in sixty days no regret · regret

Two things keep it honest. A stop is one row per stretch of waiting, not one per turn, and it closes the moment you type into the pane: that is your next move, in your words. And resolving a row is bookkeeping, not judgment: did mail arrive, did the task reopen, did the person deliver and when are facts already in the store, and the once-a-day sweep reads them without a model. What the facts mean is always the model's, at reflection.

Reflection distils it into the stores that exist. The resolved rows reach reflection as evidence lines with their own ids, out beside the mem, rv and task ids a learned line already cites. What you do goes to LEARNED.md with the same grammar, the same strength, the same decay: "a green PR from the coding agent is merged as it stands"; "when an agent asks to open the PR, you say yes". Facts about other people ("Ray answers in three days and needs a second chase") and about kinds of work ("closing from a thread with an open question reopens 40%; ask first") go elsewhere: a page per person derived from the ledger, and work facts proposed into the playbook they belong to (both below).

The one rule#

A derived line never acts on its own. It changes which button comes first, what a proposal says, what triage weighs, which day a chase goes out. Autonomy stays a proposal you click. "Stop offering" on any line writes the opposite evidence, and deleting the line from LEARNED.md is the whole undo. Earned autonomy - the Advisor proposing to do itself what you have done the same way every time, revocable from the same line - is shaped for but not built; every piece here is made so it needs nothing new.

What is built, and what is next#

Built - all five steps of the design: goals as tasks (a task whose boxes are tasks, a person as an assignee, a promise as a task on you with its day, one row per goal on the rail); the outcome ledger with its daily sweep and reflection reading it; the Advisor on the event, with the prompt-box pill and the notes in the clock menu; a page per person and a per-person day, with the slipped lane and its four answers; and triage naming which goal step a mail serves (the mail lands under that step, and may tick a box nobody holds), opening a goal for the open-ended ask with the planner proposing its steps, and work facts proposed into playbooks. Next, kept in mind but not built: earned autonomy.
