The Advisor

The Advisor is the one of Taskuary's three models that thinks about your work as a whole. Triage judges each arrival and the assistant moves things on a turn; the Advisor reads everything, on a clock, and says what should happen next: what is late, what nobody followed up, which step a mail served, what worked, which system is worth connecting - and, once a day, what would make a difference to your job itself (below: Looking out for you). It never acts. It leaves a line, an idea row or a proposal, and you or the assistant do the rest. COUNSEL.md is its voice and the assistant's both.

The operational side - the report that runs it, its schedule, the five words under an idea - is on Reports and the Advisor. This page is what goes into it, and what it learns from.

What it reads#

What one run of the Advisor report hands the model, today:

Input What it tells the Advisor
SOUL.md and JOB.md, the head of each Who you are and what matters: it weighs a slipped thread by whom it serves, and never contradicts the constitution. The chat reads both whole; the report takes the opening of each
COUNSEL.md How it speaks and how readily it takes a position
Your work as the hub sees it The threads of the last two days with your own lines marked, what arrived, the open tasks and what closed this week, who is out of office from their auto-replies
The calendar Who you meet and when: context for a meeting, and a chase that should wait for the one on Thursday
The knowledge base What agents have learned, by name, so it does not raise what is already known
The sources it watches The systems and saved data views on its report card, pulled silently each run
Its own notes What it reviewed last time and what you put down, so it never raises the same silence twice
LEARNED.md's promoted lines What your own verdicts taught, with their counts - the habits it weighs, never restates as news
The outcome ledger The last month of what came of actions, each with an id to cite, and the ledger's page on each person the run names

Two more inputs reach the Advisor at reflection, the pass that rewrites LEARNED.md, rather than on a report run: LEARNED.md itself, whole, hypotheses included, the outcome ledger below, and what you pressed while its line was shown. A matching skill reaches its line on an item (below); your mutes are designed as an input and not yet read.

None of this is copied into a prompt you cannot see. The report's Preview shows exactly what one run hands the model.

Two clocks#

On the clock, today. The Advisor report runs on its schedule and when the app opens; the morning brief runs daily; reflection runs at launch and after three corrections, and reads the ledger whenever it runs. These find what only a window shows: a promise gone quiet, a task nobody touched, a goal with no movement, a sender you keep hearing from through a system you have not connected. Their findings are rows, judged like any arrival, because a forgotten task is work waiting on you and belongs on the rail where Next reads it in turn. Nothing fires for absence; something has to look.

A rule worth making. When your own presses already draw a hard edge, the clock asks once whether to make it a rule, phrased as a question on its own idea row: "You've put down all 12 mails from newsletter@vendor.example and acted on none. Make it a rule so they are filed without reaching you? (Rules run before the AI.)" Make it a rule writes the rule under Settings → Rules, named "(learned)" and saying it was promoted from learning; Not for me is the no, and the same question comes back only once the evidence has tripled. The bars are counts, never a reading: eight threads from one sender put down (Not for me, Ignore this sender) and none taken up is an ignore rule; twenty from an automated sender is a skip; three automated senders on one domain with twelve between them is a domain rule; five of one sender's tasks marked urgent by hand, none ever put down, is an escalate rule. Never an ignore or a skip for a colleague or for anyone you have written to (an escalate rule may be - it is for exactly those people, the boss above all), never when a rule already covers them, and one such question at a time.

A report worth filing. When Taskuary itself keeps failing the same way, the clock raises one line marked worth reporting, with a Report this? move. It opens the Report popup, which shows the scrubbed draft and opens GitHub's new-issue page for your Submit. The line goes up after three agent runs end with the same error this week on at least two tasks, or after two since an update that brought the failure. It also goes up when the Assistant's fix-it check gives up on the same connection twice in two weeks. These are counts, not the AI's reading: a refused sign-in is yours to fix, and a task with words from someone you have not vouched for is never read. Each problem is raised once, and Not for me keeps it down (Troubleshooting).

Looking out for you. Once a day the Advisor stops thinking about the app and thinks about your job, the way a sharp chief of staff would: what could you tell your boss - "I see this pain, and here is the fix"? It reads JOB.md and SOUL.md, the last 30 days of mail, chat, tasks and reports counted and grouped (a report that comes every Monday, a question asked again and again, a notice that keeps failing), how often the people you write to most wrote each week, and what it raised before and what you did with it. It proposes at most two ideas, from five families:

Family What it looks for
Credit Work you did that leadership likely does not see, with the numbers, and a way to show it
Pitch A pain other people keep having - the same question, the same report fixed by hand, the same vendor problem - and a fix you could build or propose
Money Money or time left on the table: a duplicate charge or record, a credit owed, an unused licence, a late fee, a retirement date with a cost on it - the work version of asking for a refund when a price dropped
Early warning A problem developing: a job that will fail again on a date it can name, a report that stopped coming, a deadline at risk, a colleague whose pattern changed sharply
Leverage Who depends on you, who to loop in, an introduction that would unblock something

Each idea says what was seen (counts and dates), the messages and tasks it rests on, why it matters, the next step, and the one sentence you could say to your boss. Then a second, short look reads each idea beside the messages it cites and keeps only what is specific, supported, not something one message already told you, and worth a boss's time. Generic advice - "communicate more", "hold a meeting", "document the process" - never gets through. An idea about a person states only what changed (counts, dates), never a diagnosis or a motive, and its next step is private and kind. No word list decides any of this: the two prompts are the bar, and the code checks only what it can - every message or task an idea cites was really in what the Advisor read, and nothing rests on a personal workstream.

What passes is an ordinary idea row: Send to agent has an agent draft the pitch or build the fix (its brief carries the next step, the pitch and the messages to read first), Make a task, Remind me, Not for me, and No more ideas like this, which stops that whole family. What you did with each idea, and what the second look held back, is what the next day's pass reads first - it never raises again what it said before; each family is held to the same 80% bar as the Advisor's other kinds of line. It costs two calls on the Advisor's model a day (three at most, when an answer has to be asked for again), it does not look again while two of its ideas still wait on you, and the setting lookout_every_days changes how often it looks. The Advisor checks every 15 minutes whether a pass is due, separately from its report runs. Switching the Advisor report off under Reports stops the pass too.

Asking for a pass now. You don't have to wait for the day to come round. Ask the Assistant - look for ideas for my job now, or run the advisor ideas - and it starts a pass in the background. The reply says it started; when the pass finishes, a second line in the same chat says how many ideas it raised and how many the second look held back. The ideas land under Advisor like any others. Two rules still apply: if two of its ideas are already waiting, nothing starts and the reply says so. Answer one of them and ask again. With no AI connected, nothing starts either. Asking for a pass also restarts the day's count, so the next scheduled pass comes a day after this one.

It lights no button. The Advisor speaks on its own rows under Advisor, and nowhere else: it never fills a button - not on a task's row, not on its own idea rows, not on the Tasks tab or the phone. A task's row fills a button only where the move is already settled, in plain code: a draft or held action waiting for your yes fills Send, and an agent that said nothing is left fills Mark done. Everything else - a late wait, a failure, work unpushed, an ask nobody took - fills nothing: what to do next is the task's agent's to suggest, on its own card. An idea's row fills nothing either.

What it knows about how you work still sets up a few buttons, each counted from what you did:

  • Whom you hand that kind of ask to. Once you have handed a sender's asks to the same person twice, Hand it to a person opens with that person already in To, saying whose usual they are. They are chosen, not sent: nothing goes until you press Send it.
  • The day you put it off to. When you keep putting a sender's mail off to the same weekday, the memory writes it as a line under How you work in LEARNED.md, such as You put mail from renewals@payworth.example off to Friday. It climbs the same ladder as any other line: it waits in Watching, and is in use after three put-offs over two weeks. Remind me reads its day from that line. It opens with that day first, filled, saying whose habit it is, and the line shows on hover. Reword the day in the line and Remind me follows. Delete the line, or mark it Wrong, and there is no learned day. Nothing is set until you press it.
  • A person's own day. A wait is late after three working days - its agent is woken to chase it; once three of someone's answers say how soon they answer, it is late on their day instead: Gail usually answers in a day; this is 3 days.

What it learns from. No button press is kept or learned from: a press is not a fact about how you work. What LEARNED.md knows comes from what you did and wrote, in context - who wrote, what they are to you, how soon you answered, what you put off and until when, whom you handed it to, what you closed without a reply, what you typed to an agent. It keeps what that means, never the number: answering someone within minutes, again and again, is written as that person matters, and a kind of mail you close without a reply as not yours to answer - and triage reads both on the next arrival. What an agent concluded is never a fact about you.

What it brings up is yours to teach. The clock reads what you took up and what you said no to: the ideas you dismissed, and What to bring up in LEARNED.md. An idea of a kind you said no to again and again stays unsaid, however it is worded - three "unsubscribe from this newsletter" ideas put down, and the fourth newsletter gets none. A promise you keep on a schedule - the same person, the same weekday, three weeks or more - is raised when its day has passed with nothing sent to them: The headcount sheet you send Gail on Mondays hasn't gone yet. Anything you send them puts it down, and so does No, for that week.

Habits learn themselves. Nothing asks you to confirm what was learned: a habit goes into use once three of your own acts - presses, approvals, edits, answers - bear it out, and an act that goes against it takes it back out until they do again. The memory keeps conclusions only, a few lines per person and a short list in all; the oldest fall out first. Wrong on the Memory page, or saying so to the Assistant ("the one about sign-in help is not right"), takes a line out at once.

How it is tested. tests/rail_advisor_cases.py is the eval: every kind of idea it puts on the rail (a follow-up, a promise, work gone quiet, the app's health, a system worth connecting, its own idea, a rule worth making), each in a world where it should fire and in worlds where it should not, plus the demo world five days on, checked twice with every line put down between. A line that fires where it should not, or fires and is not useful - about automated mail, back after a No, too early, the wrong person, stale, over an agent at work - fails the suite with its reason.

What was not followed up#

A thread you wrote on that nobody answered, or a step somebody owes past its day, is slipped once it has been quiet past that person's usual day: half again their median answer, once the ledger has seen them answer three times, and the default from Settings → Advisor until then. Someone whose auto-reply says they are away never slips. A slipped row sits inside Tasks, the pipe count says "3 slipped", and when Next brings it up the card offers four answers, each an outcome the ledger keeps:

  • Chase them drafts a short nudge on the thread, in your voice, for your yes - copying whoever the ledger has seen get them to answer, when it has. When it goes out, the reply is marked a chase, and how long the answer took is counted against them.
  • Let it go puts it down: the step is dropped, or the follow-up closed. Three Let-it-gos on one person and the Advisor stops raising that person; nothing else changes.
  • They answered elsewhere closes the step as done (its box ticks) and keeps where, in your words.
  • Remind me is the same Remind me as everywhere else.

A reply arriving ends it on its own: the follow-up closes and the step leaves the lane. The morning brief carries the same facts, one line each, with the person's day named.

A page per person#

Facts about other people go to a page per person, derived from the ledger at reflection and kept under About you → Docs → People by address: how fast they answer, whether a chase is needed, who on copy moves them, what they deliver late - each line with the evidence ids it rests on, and the counted numbers underneath. The lines are the model's reading; the numbers are the ledger's. Your own lines above the block are yours and survive every recompile. The page is read wherever the hub's dossier is read: the reply drafter, triage's context, meeting prep, and the Advisor's bigger read.

Facts about kinds of work go to the skill they belong to, as a proposal: once a skill has five closes on the ledger, reflection writes a bullet block - how often a close came open again, and how often the ones closed with a question still open did - and it waits for your yes on the Review queue. Your edits teach it too: when you change an agent's draft before sending it, on a task worked from a skill, the next reflection reads what was drafted beside what you sent and suggests the update to that skill as the same kind of card. Nothing edits a skill on its own; your own lines on it are never touched.

The outcome ledger#

Every store Taskuary keeps is written from one signal: your click or your sentence, at the moment you give it. LEARNED.md is what you do when you correct; the verdict and routing memories stop at the verdict. Nothing recorded what you did next, or what the world did after Taskuary acted. That is what the ledger records: one row per action the app took or proposed, filled in two halves.

The action, recorded when it happens What the sweep looks for Verdict
A reply sent (approved as drafted, or edited first) More material mail from them on the thread in a week. An auto-reply and a calendar response are not rounds; your own later mail is not theirs settled · unsettled: N more
A task closed, by any road Is it still closed a week on held · reopened
An agent stopped (with or without a pull request open) Your next move: a continue, with the words you typed into the pane or the session, or a close; or nothing in two days continued · closed · left
A pull request merged Two weeks on, nothing says otherwise held
A promise you made in a sent mail, as a task on you with its day Delivered when, against the day you named; or a week past it with nothing kept · kept late · late
A sender ignored by a rule Whether their mail became a task anyway in sixty days no regret · regret

Two things keep it honest. A stop is one row per stretch of waiting, not one per turn, and it closes the moment you type into the pane: that is your next move, in your words. And resolving a row is bookkeeping, not judgment: did mail arrive, did the task reopen, did the person deliver and when are facts already in the store, and the once-a-day sweep reads them without a model. What the facts mean is always the model's, at reflection.

Reflection distils it into the stores that exist. The resolved rows reach reflection as evidence lines with their own ids, out beside the mem, rv and task ids a learned line already cites. What you do goes to LEARNED.md with the same grammar, the same strength, the same decay: "a green PR from the coding agent is merged as it stands"; "when an agent asks to open the PR, you say yes". Facts about other people ("Ray answers in three days and needs a second chase") and about kinds of work ("closing from a thread with an open question reopens 40%; ask first") go elsewhere: a page per person derived from the ledger, and work facts proposed into the skill they belong to (both below).

The one rule#

A derived line never acts on its own. It changes which button comes first, what a proposal says, what triage weighs, which day a chase goes out. Autonomy stays a proposal you click. "Stop offering" on any line writes the opposite evidence, and deleting the line from LEARNED.md is the whole undo. Earned autonomy - the Advisor proposing to do itself what you have done the same way every time, revocable from the same line - is shaped for but not built; every piece here is made so it needs nothing new.

What is built, and what is next#

Built - all five steps of the design: goals as tasks (a task whose boxes are tasks, a person as an assignee, a promise as a task on you with its day, one row per goal on the rail); the outcome ledger with its daily sweep and reflection reading it; the Advisor on the event, with the prompt-box pill and the notes in the clock menu; a page per person and a per-person day, with the slipped lane and its four answers; and triage naming which goal step a mail serves (the mail lands under that step, and may tick a box nobody holds), opening a goal for the open-ended ask with the planner proposing its steps, and work facts proposed into skills. Next, kept in mind but not built: earned autonomy.
