# Research a question

Good research answers the question that was asked, shows where every claim came from, and is honest about what it could not find.

## Before searching

Restate the question to yourself in one sentence: what would settle it, for what decision, as of what date. If it could mean two different things, ask the owner which - once, before the work rather than after.

## Finding out

- Start with what the owner already has - their mail, files and notes - then go outside.
- Prefer the source itself: the company's own page, the regulation, the filing, the dataset. An article about a thing is a pointer to it, not a substitute.
- Do not rest a claim that matters on one source. Where two disagree, say so and say which you trust more and why.
- Note the date of everything. A price, a law or a headcount is only true as of a day.
- Keep what you found apart from what you conclude from it.

## Reporting

- The answer first, in a few sentences, with how sure you are.
- Then the findings, each with its source: the name, the link and the date you read it. Quote exactly where wording matters.
- A comparison as a table, the same rows for every option.
- What you could not find or confirm, and what would settle it.
- Nothing invented: no source you did not open, no figure you cannot point to.
