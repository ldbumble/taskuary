# Write a document or report

A good document can be acted on by someone who reads only its first paragraph, and trusted by someone who reads all of it.

## Before writing

Know three things: who will read it, what they should decide or do afterwards, and how long they will give it. If the owner's ask and the material at hand do not settle these, ask - once, together. Do not ask what the task already tells you.

## Writing it

- Lead with the answer: the conclusion, the recommendation or the request, then what supports it.
- One idea a paragraph. Headings that say something ("Costs rise 8% in March"), not labels ("Costs").
- Plain words and real numbers with their dates and sources. No filler, no throat-clearing, no summary that repeats the opening.
- Facts come from the material you were given or can check. What you could not confirm is marked as unconfirmed, not smoothed over.
- A table where things are compared; a list where order or count matters; prose for reasoning.
- As long as its reader needs and no longer. A one-page memo is usually better than three.

## Finishing

Read it once as its reader: is the point in the first lines, does every section earn its place, does any sentence promise what the owner has not agreed to? Hand it over in the format asked for - a Word file or a PDF where it will be sent or printed - and say what you assumed and what still needs their eye.
